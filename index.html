Why Artie

Same problem, two mechanisms

Getting database changes out reliably as events. I have built one working answer to this. Artie builds the other. Here is exactly where they meet and where they do not.

What I built, the outbox pattern

In Sama, the application writes an event in the same transaction as the change it describes, into an outbox table. A relay reads that table and publishes to Kafka, with redelivery deduplicated on the inbox side. The database change and the event never disagree, because they are the same write.

What Artie builds, change data capture

A CDC connector reads the database's own replication log directly, the WAL in Postgres or the binlog in MySQL, after the change has already happened. The application never writes anything extra, and never knows the capture is happening at all. That is a lower level, protocol specific problem, closer to distributed systems and storage engine internals than to application design.

What is genuinely shared

Ordering guarantees. Exactly once semantics downstream. Schema evolution as the source table changes shape. Backpressure when the destination cannot keep up with the source. I have real, production depth in all four of these, just from the outbox side of the problem.

What is genuinely different

Outbox requires the application to participate, one extra write, inside one transaction. CDC requires no application participation at all, and instead requires understanding a specific database's replication protocol well enough to build a connector against it. That second skill, I do not have yet.

HONEST GAP

I have not built a native CDC connector, and I would rather learn that skill in the open, on a team that already has it well established, than imply I already have it. What I bring instead is real production experience with the downstream half of the same pipeline, Kafka at high throughput, Postgres tuned under real load, and the outbox side of exactly once delivery.
